Step 1 · Map institutional context

Understand the institution before discussing solutions

Begin with the institution’s mandate, operating context and stakeholder ecosystem. The objective is not to jump immediately to a product. First establish how the institution creates public value, how decisions are made and which parties influence delivery.

Institution Metro Infrastructure & Services Agency

A fictional public entity used for this learning case.

Mandate Coordinate and deliver priority urban infrastructure programmes

Focus areas include transport access, water resilience and municipal service upgrades.

Operating context Multi-year capital programme with several delivery partners

Execution depends on public budgets, procurement, contractors and intergovernmental coordination.

Current challenge Timing mismatch between approved expenditure and supplier payment cycles

Management is concerned about programme delays and pressure across the supplier ecosystem.

Your briefing

You are preparing to support a relationship manager before an introductory stakeholder meeting. Your task is to frame the institution in context before proposing questions, risks or banking needs.

Step 2 · Diagnose fiscal dynamics

Follow the money, timing and constraints

Public-sector financial needs cannot be understood from annual budget totals alone. Consider where revenue comes from, when funding becomes available, when expenditure falls due, and how capital-programme execution affects liquidity.

Funding profile Transfers, own-source revenue and project-specific capital allocations

Not all funding arrives at the same time or with the same degree of flexibility.

Expenditure profile Large contractor and supplier payments linked to project milestones

Cash requirements can cluster around delivery and certification cycles.

Timing issue Approved expenditure can precede the practical availability of cash

This can create short-term pressure even where the programme is fully budgeted.

Management concern Delay in payments could slow delivery and place smaller suppliers under pressure

The problem is operational as well as financial.

Which hypothesis is most useful to test?

Choose the interpretation that best connects fiscal structure to the institution’s operating challenge.

AI Coach Correct direction. Budget approval and cash availability are not the same thing. The useful next step is to understand the timing, restrictions and predictability of cash flows before drawing conclusions about financing.

Step 3 · Connect needs and risk

Move from context to informed hypotheses

Your objective is not to decide the solution before the client conversation. Instead, develop evidence-based hypotheses about potential needs, risks and areas requiring specialist input.

Possible banking need Cash visibility and liquidity management

Relevant if timing mismatches are recurring and predictable.

Possible banking need Payments and supplier ecosystem support

Relevant where high-volume or milestone-linked payments affect programme delivery.

Risk question What governance and approvals govern access to funding or banking facilities?

Public-sector mandates, policy boundaries and delegated authority matter.

Execution question Are delays caused by liquidity, approvals, procurement or project execution?

Different root causes require different responses.

Which is the strongest next position?

Select the most credible way to frame potential opportunity without overreaching.

AI Coach This keeps the discussion mandate-led and diagnostic. It connects potential banking needs to evidence while recognising governance, policy and execution boundaries.
Step 4 · Lead with guidance

Prepare for a credible stakeholder conversation

A good discovery conversation should test your hypotheses rather than simply confirm them. Frame questions that connect institutional mandate, fiscal dynamics, operational delivery and banking needs.

Mandate question Which programmes and service outcomes are most exposed if payment timing deteriorates?
Fiscal question How predictable are major inflows relative to contractor and supplier payment cycles?
Governance question Which approvals and policy constraints shape available liquidity or transaction options?
Opportunity question Where could better cash visibility, payments execution or specialist support reduce friction?
AI
AI Coach guidance What makes a strong question?

Strong questions are open, evidence-seeking and tied to the institution’s operating model. Avoid questions that assume a specific product is already the answer.
